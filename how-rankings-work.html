How rankings work

Your list, and how everyone’s lists become the Global one.

Your list

Your ranking is yours: best to worst, in whatever order you put it. Nothing on the site reorders it for you.

You can drag rides into place, but the easier way is Rank more. It asks you one question at a time: which was better? Each answer halves the part of your list the ride could still go in, so even a 500-ride list takes only around ten questions. Once it has narrowed down a spot, it checks the ride against its neighbors above and below before placing it.

If two rides are honestly a toss-up, tap Can’t decide and the new one goes in right below the ride you were comparing it with.

What Rank more asks about

  • Mostly your tallest unranked credits first, since those tend to be the ones you have the strongest feelings about.
  • Every third pick is a category you haven’t placed yet, until they’re all done. Once you have 100+ ranked, new categories come first, because a category you haven’t placed by then was probably just added.
  • Once you have 20 ranked, some of those every-third picks become rides other riders have ranked. Rides on only one list come first, since your ranking is what gives them a Global place.

Categories

Some rides are near-identical copies: Vekoma Boomerangs, SLCs, Wacky Worms. A category puts all of them on one row, so you rank the design once instead of 20 times. Placing a category places every ride from it you’ve been on, and one you ride later joins that row without another question. If you had already ranked some of them on their own, you’ll be asked whether to gather them together.

The Global list

The Global list combines every rider’s ranking into one score out of 100. Four ideas go into it.

  1. Where it sits among your credits. A ride’s score on your list depends on how far down it is, measured against how many coasters you’ve ridden. Your #1 scores 100. Rides you haven’t ranked count as below everything you have, so if you’ve ridden 800 but only ranked your top 100, your #100 still scores 87.6. You don’t need to rank your back half for your top half to count fully.
  2. More credits, more say. Each rider’s vote is weighted by the square root of their credit count, capped at 400 credits: 25 credits weighs 5, 100 weighs 10, 400 or more weighs 20. Experience counts, but nobody outvotes everyone else.
  3. The odd low score is dropped. Once a ride is on 5 lists, its lowest score is left out. From 20 lists, the lowest tenth are (2 of 20, 3 of 30). One rider who had a bad day on it can’t sink it.
  4. A light pull toward the middle. Every ride gets a small extra vote at the site-wide average, worth about a quarter of a typical rider. A ride on three lists can’t jump straight to #1 on three perfect scores, but a ride everyone who’s been on loves still ranks high.

A ride needs to be on at least 2 lists to get a Global place, and a list counts once it has 10 rides ranked. Ties go to the ride on more lists.

Example: one ride, on three lists.

RiderCreditsRanked itScoreWeight
A400#499.320
B64#1085.98
C25#296.05

The weighted average is 95.5. The pull toward the middle (here, an extra weight of 3 at an average of 50) brings it to 91.7. With only three lists, nothing is dropped.

“You agree on”

On another rider’s list, you’ll see how much you agree. It takes every pair of rides you’ve both ranked and counts how often you put them in the same order. The biggest split is the ride where your two lists are furthest apart, measured as how far down each list it sits.
